# Maintenance and communication

NCSC Code themes 3 and 4 cover vulnerability handling, updates, support and
communication. Their vendor/customer provisions require applicability assessment;
see [sources](sources.md).

Establish who receives a vulnerability report, assesses exposure, owns remediation
and communicates with affected consumers. Use existing work tracking and communication
channels. Keep sensitive vulnerability detail out of public issues until disclosure
is authorized and appropriately coordinated.

Assess a finding against actual versions, deployment conditions and reachable
behavior. Identify the affected obligation, available evidence, mitigation and
remaining uncertainty. Prioritize using the project's risk context; do not invent
universal remediation deadlines or silently accept residual risk for the owner.

For an update or recovery path, verify the relevant behavior and identify affected
consumers. A prepared fix, a published release, consumer adoption and verified
remediation are distinct states. Preserve evidence of earlier failures.

Support commitments and customer notices belong to the party able to make those
commitments. Do not apply commercial vendor promises automatically to a local tool
or open-source library. If the organization adopts the full Code, assess its exact
provisions rather than treating this short guide as a conformity checklist.
